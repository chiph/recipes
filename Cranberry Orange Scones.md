# Cranberry Orange Scones

## Method 1

### Ingredients

* 270 g all-purpose flour
* 7 g baking powder
* 5 g salt
* 64 g sugar
* 6 tbsp. (85 g / 3 oz) unsalted butter, frozen
* 3/4 cup (177 g / 6.25 oz) heavy cream, chilled
* 2 eggs, beaten
* Zest of 2 oranges
* 1/3 cup dried cranberries, chopped

### Instructions

1. Mix the cream, eggs, and orange zest ahead of time so the zest flavor comes through. I usually mix these the night before, but a few hours is probably plenty.
2. Mix or sift the flour, baking powder, salt, and sugar in a bowl.
3. Cut the butter into pieces and add it to the flour mixture. Cut the butter into the flour mixture until combined.
4. At this point, I usually throw the bowl into the freezer overnight—you want the butter cold when you bake the scones. (Again, this is probably more time than needed.)
5. Preheat the oven to 375°F.
6. Make a well in the flour mixture and pour in the cream mixture. Mix using a wooden spoon or rubber spatula.
7. Mix in the cranberries.
8. Knead the dough on a floured surface such as wax paper or a Silpat. The dough will be sticky, so add only enough flour to make it workable.
9. Roll out into a 1-inch-thick disc or square and cut into 8 triangles. You can use a biscuit or cookie cutter too.
10. Put the scones onto an ungreased baking sheet 1 inch apart.
11. Bake for 23–25 minutes, until golden brown.
12. Cool on a rack.

## Method 2

This one is slightly easier, and I think a bit better.

### Ingredients

* 2 cups (250 g) all-purpose flour, spooned and leveled, plus more for hands and work surface
* 1/2 cup (100 g) granulated sugar
* 2 1/2 teaspoons baking powder
* 1/2 teaspoon salt
* 115 g (1 stick) unsalted butter, frozen
* 1/2 cup (120 ml) heavy cream, plus 2 Tbsp for brushing
* 1 large egg
* 1 1/2 teaspoons pure vanilla extract

### Variations

#### Cranberry-orange

* 1 cup dried cranberries, chopped
* Zest of 2 oranges

#### Blueberry

* 1 heaping cup (180 g) fresh or frozen blueberries (do not thaw)
* 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon

### Instructions

* Whisk flour, sugar, baking powder, cinnamon, and salt together in a large bowl. Grate the frozen butter using a box grater. Add it to the flour mixture and combine with a pastry cutter, two forks, or your fingers until the mixture comes together.
* Whisk 1/2 cup heavy cream, the egg, and vanilla extract together in a small bowl. If making the cranberry-orange scones, add zest.
* Drizzle the wet mixture over the flour mixture, add the blueberries or cranberries, then mix together until everything appears moistened.
* Pour onto the counter and, with floured hands, work the dough into a ball as best you can. Dough will be sticky. If it’s too sticky, add a little more flour. If it seems too dry, add 1–2 more tablespoons of heavy cream.
* Brush scones with remaining heavy cream and, for extra crunch, sprinkle with coarse sugar. (You can do this before or after refrigerating in the next step.)
* Place scones on a plate or lined baking sheet, if your fridge has space, and refrigerate for at least 15 minutes.
* Meanwhile, preheat the oven to 400°F (204°C).
* Line a large baking sheet with parchment paper or a silicone baking mat. After refrigerating, arrange scones 2–3 inches apart on the prepared baking sheet(s).
* Bake for 22–25 minutes or until golden brown around the edges and lightly browned on top. Remove from the oven and cool for a few minutes before topping with vanilla icing.
* Leftover iced or un-iced scones keep well at room temperature for 2 days or in the refrigerator for 5 days.

### Notes

* You want the butter in the batter to be *cold*, so I tend to stash the flour mixture in the freezer overnight. About 30 minutes is *probably* enough.
* I combine the cream, egg, vanilla and zest at the same time to give the orange zest more time to infuse.
* 25 minutes is likely too long—start checking at 22 minutes.
